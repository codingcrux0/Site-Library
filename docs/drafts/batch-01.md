## Descriptions and catches

1. **Landdding** — A gallery of landing page and website designs from Webflow, Framer, WordPress and more, with templates and a colour browser.
   Catch: The free plan limits the feed, search and filters (the limit isn't stated), shows partner ads and can't submit sites. PRO is $4 a month.
2. **Collect UI** — A daily-updated gallery of hand-picked interface designs, many from Dribbble, sorted by what they show.
   Catch: Much of the page is sponsor links, and the designs are pictures to learn from, not components you can copy.
3. **Inspora** — A visual gallery of web, branding, product, motion, illustration, 3D and print designs, updated often.
   Catch: Posts show pictures and titles only, with no descriptions, credits or usage terms, so you can't tell who made a design or if you may reuse it.
4. **Footer** — A gallery of website footer designs you can filter by style (typographic, grid, animated, dark and more).
   Catch: It shows footers only, it doesn't say how designs are chosen, and at least one entry is marked Sponsored.
5. **TapMac** — A Mac app that starts dictation, AI text actions or clipboard history when you tap your MacBook.
   Catch: It is paid ($7.99 at launch, then $20) with no free trial, only 5 days to get a refund; it needs an Apple silicon Mac on macOS 14 or later, and the AI actions need macOS 26.
6. **ReactDrops** — React components, animations and visual effects you can preview, adjust and copy into your project.
   Catch: The page shows no licence, price or install steps, and says nothing about which React versions it supports.
7. **Doblique** — 27 free browser tools for PDFs, images, text, code and calculators, with no sign-up.
   Catch: Most tools take files up to 100 MB (250 MB for splitting and deleting PDF pages), and "files never leave your browser" is the site's own claim.
8. **isthereanytool?** — A searchable directory of nearly 1,900 design, craft and AI tools in 20 categories, with guides and comparisons.
   Catch: Sponsored placements sit among the results, and listings show no dates, so you can't tell how current they are.
9. **Iter0** — An AI builder that turns a product brief into an editable launch page, with an API for developers.
   Catch: Its page shows no pricing or free tier, and the API needs keys it doesn't explain how to get.
10. **Backgrounds Supply** — A library of backgrounds, wallpapers and gradients sold as a one-time $49 purchase.
    Catch: There is no free tier, no refunds on the digital product, and you can't resell the images as your own pack; its own counts disagree (1,273 or 1,167 backgrounds).
11. **v0 Templates** — A library of community-made starter templates (apps, landing pages, dashboards, login screens) from the v0 AI builder.
    Catch: The page doesn't show prices or what the free plan allows, and you need an account to use a template.
12. **beUI** — About 150 animated React components plus React Native components, blocks and charts you copy in with the shadcn CLI.
    Catch: The MIT free library sits beside a separate paid Pro collection (price not shown), and it targets React 19 and Tailwind 4 so older projects need work.
13. **Mobbin** — A searchable library of real app and website screenshots, screens and user flows for design research.
    Catch: Most of the library is behind paid plans, the free plan is limited (its limits aren't shown), and the AI-agent connection needs a paid plan.
14. **Navbar Gallery** — A gallery of navigation bar designs sorted by type: static, dropdown, mega menu, sidebar and more.
    Catch: It covers navbars only, is sponsored by Mobbin with banners throughout, and shows no terms for reusing the designs.
15. **60fps** — About 2,100 short clips of real app interactions (swipes, loaders, onboarding) from iOS and web apps, with filters.
    Catch: The clips are for learning, not code (only a small Snippets section has any), and a PRO tier exists that the page doesn't explain.
16. **Motion** — A catalogue of animated web sections (heroes, galleries, product showcases), some free and some paid.
    Catch: Paid sections show no price on the list, several are heavy (WebGL and canvas), and some effects are simplified in Safari and Firefox.
17. **Wedoflow** — A shop of about 200 premium Webflow templates, with an All-Access pass.
    Catch: Templates are $24 to $169 and downloads need a subscription; some listings contradict themselves (marked "free" but priced $79).
18. **EasyUI** — Open-source React components such as an interactive area chart, a gooey menu and a liquid toggle.
    Catch: The landing page shows no licence, install steps or version, so check its repository before using it in client work.
19. **Bencho** — An index of 100+ interactive React components and micro-interactions such as sliders, loaders and drag-and-drop cards.
    Catch: The page lists names only, with no previews, licence, price or install steps, so you have to open each block to judge it.
20. **Wensity UI** — 112 animated React components, 57 UI primitives and 82 page blocks you copy into your own project, plus free gradient and noise tools.
    Catch: Only 38 components are free; the rest need Pro ($169 a year or $199 once), purchases are final, and it needs React 18.2, TypeScript 5 and Tailwind 4.
21. **Foldwell (uberdesigner.ai)** — A paid animated template of a dark file-manager screen, with a prompt for AI coding tools and a Vite and React project.
    Catch: It costs $29 a month or $249 a year with no free tier, you can't resell or share the files, and the Figma file is still "coming soon".
22. **PanelUI** — 141 accessible React Native components for Expo, styled with Tailwind and copied in with a command-line tool.
    Catch: It is for Expo and React Native only (not websites), the page shows no licence or price, and the AI features are in beta.
23. **remove.bg** — An online tool that removes the background from a photo automatically, with apps, an API and a Photoshop extension.
    Catch: The "free" use is limited (the page doesn't say how) and top quality needs a paid plan; images you rate may be used to improve the service unless you opt out.

24. **ReUI** — A shadcn/ui-based library with over 1,200 free components and 27 custom pieces (data grid, kanban, filters) you copy in, plus paid Pro blocks, icons and templates.
    Catch: Pro blocks and templates start at $249 once, some "free" templates need an account and aren't MIT, and you must set up shadcn/ui first.
25. **21st.dev** — A community catalogue of React and Tailwind components, templates and themes (it claims over 12,000) with live previews and one-command install.
    Catch: Free use is limited to 2 component copies a day, unlimited copies and premium templates need a paid membership (price not shown), and quality varies because anyone can publish.
26. **Spectrum UI** — Animated React components, blocks and charts built on shadcn/ui, Tailwind and Motion, installable through an MCP server for AI tools.
    Catch: Its counts disagree (250+ on the home page, 58 in the docs), and I couldn't read its FAQ, so what is free and what the licence allows isn't confirmed.
27. **Transitions.dev** — 43+ ready-made UI motion transitions with remix links, plus an AI builder and a tool that scores a codebase's motion.
    Catch: Many transitions are Pro-only (no price on the page I read), no licence for the code is stated, and the page didn't fully load for me.
28. **Kinetics** — 162 interface animation examples for CSS and React (buttons, toggles, tabs, drag, loaders, text effects) you preview and copy.
    Catch: Not every effect has React code, some snippets are partial, a paid Pro version is only on a waitlist, and no licence is stated on the page.
29. **8bitcn** — Retro 8-bit styled components and blocks you copy and paste; open source.
    Catch: The page names no licence or framework and shows no dates, so check its GitHub repository before using it.
30. **Evil Charts** — Animated, interactive chart components for React and shadcn/ui, built on Recharts and ECharts, that you copy into your project.
    Catch: The page shows no price, licence or update date, and you inherit the limits of Recharts and ECharts.
31. **Designeer** — A curated catalogue of interface craft, component libraries, AI tools and resources, with a feed of recent designs from individual creators.
    Catch: Sponsored placements are prominent, the site is marked "All Rights Reserved" with no reuse terms, and it has accounts without saying what is free.
32. **affectUI** — 129 React components, screens and blocks for AI products and SaaS (agent monitors, approval flows, chat composers), each a single file.
    Catch: Only 40 items are free (MIT), the rest is Pro from $199 once, and the page doesn't say which items are the free ones.
